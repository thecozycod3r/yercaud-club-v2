A club of
the Shevaroys.

Founded by coffee planters in 1897, Yercaud Club has been a meeting place in the hills for more than 125 years.

Through the years

THE SHEVAROY HILLS

Hills that rise
from the plains.

The Shevaroys rise steeply from the hot plains of Tamil Nadu to 1,624 metres, part of the Eastern Ghats.

The range covers 384 square kilometres and 67 tribal villages. Mild temperatures, high elevation and rain shared evenly between the southwest and northeast monsoons make it ideal for coffee, pepper and fruit, and most of the hills’ people work with what the estates grow.

The native people of the hills are the Malayalis, the Tamil for “hill tribes”. Little is recorded of how they came here. One legend tells of farmers from the banks of the Cauvery who, led by one Shervarayan, fled Tipu Sultan’s armies in the late 1700s and settled on these hills. The range became Shervarayan Malai, later anglicised to the Shevaroy Hills.

31 MAY 1897

A place to meet,
play and talk.

Coffee planters had worked the Shevaroys since the 1860s. Their families wanted somewhere to meet: to play billiards, tennis and cards. So five planters formed a committee and founded the Yercaud Club, with 34 other ordinary members, in a rented house.

The first purchase was a billiard table from the Army and Navy Stores, Bombay, for ₹1,000. A piano was rented from Messrs Misquith and Co., Bangalore, at ₹20 a month. Billiards, cards, a reading room and tennis filled the days, and the club soon became the centre of social life in the hills.

Through the years.

  1. 1860s

    Coffee comes to the hills

    European planters begin coffee plantations on the Shevaroys.

  2. 31 May 1897

    The club is founded

    Five coffee planters form a committee and establish the Yercaud Club with 34 other ordinary members, in a rented house.

  3. 22 July 1897

    Building on trust

    Members borrow ₹3,000 through debentures of ₹100 each at 6% a year. The committee personally guarantees the loan and its interest.

  4. 1938

    A clubhouse of its own

    The club buys 2.01 acres of land and builds the new clubhouse.

  5. 1949

    The first Indian member

    M.S.P. Rajes is admitted. Until then, membership was limited to European planters.

  6. 1950s–1970s

    The quiet years

    Planters sell their estates and leave Yercaud from the mid-1950s. Membership falls and the club stays largely dormant through the 1960s and 70s.

  7. 1981

    Nine members

    Only nine members remain. Numbers begin to rise again in the late 1980s.

  8. 1990s

    Club life returns

    The club becomes active again, its standards kept throughout the years of stagnation.

  9. 23 August 1997

    The centenary

    Yercaud Club celebrates one hundred years.

  10. 26 March 2022

    A new tennis court

    A synthetic tennis court is commissioned with contributions from members.

  11. 30 June 2023

    The 125th year

    The club marks 125 years, improving its facilities and opening new ones.

THE 125TH YEAR

Built by
its members.

The synthetic tennis court, renovated chambers, a refurbished billiards table and table tennis were all made possible by members. For the 125th year, these facilities were added, and the club acknowledges each with gratitude.
